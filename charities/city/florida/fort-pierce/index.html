Tax-exempt organizations in Fort Pierce, Florida

569 organizations

The IRS file has 569 tax-exempt organizations with an address in Fort Pierce, Florida.

What kinds of organizations

By field of work, 120 have no code and the coded ones are mostly religion-related (110), human services (60), education (53), youth development (32), animal-related (30), philanthropy, voluntarism and grantmaking foundations (24), arts, culture and humanities (22). By type of exemption the largest groups are charitable, educational, religious and similar organization (516), post or organization of war veterans (12), fraternal beneficiary society (10). The share of Fort Pierce in the state's 115,205 organizations is 0%.

How large they report themselves to be

All 569 organizations in Fort Pierce carry an income code from the latest return. Of those, 3% report income under $25,000, 17% report $25,000 to $499,999, 7% report $500,000 to $4,999,999 and 3% report $5 million or more; 71% have income code 0, which the IRS table lists as $0; that covers organizations that reported no income and probably also organizations that have no return data in the file, so the file alone cannot tell the two apart. The bands are the IRS's own income codes for the most recent return, and a return can be a few years old. The IRS information sheet says amounts are missing for organizations that file only the small e-Postcard or that are not required to file.

Gifts, legal form and group ties

The file marks contributions as deductible for 532 of the organizations (93%) and as not deductible for 31 (5%). By type of organization in the file: 489 corporations, 76 associations, 3 trusts. 485 are independent organizations and 83 are subordinate units in a group ruling, such as a local branch of a larger body.

The foundation code, which the IRS uses for 501(c)(3) organizations, marks 331 organizations that get a substantial part of their support from the public or government, 144 churches, 24 private foundations, 12 schools, 4 supporting organizations. These are the IRS's own classes and the file does not describe an organization's work.

When the exemption was recognised

The ruling date is the month and year of the IRS letter that recognised the exemption, which is not the year the organization was founded. 260 of the 569 organizations with a ruling date (46%) were recognised in 2016 or later. The decades with the most rulings are the 2020s (188), the 2010s (164), the 2000s (75).

By filing requirement code: 112 must file Form 990 or 990-EZ, 283 need only file the small Form 990-N (income under $50,000 a year, in the IRS's words), and 174 are marked as not required to file (churches, religious organizations, government bodies and others).
