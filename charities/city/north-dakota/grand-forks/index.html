Tax-exempt organizations in Grand Forks, North Dakota

412 organizations

The IRS file has 412 tax-exempt organizations with an address in Grand Forks, North Dakota.

What kinds of organizations

By field of work, 168 have no code and the coded ones are mostly education (40), arts, culture and humanities (27), recreation and sports (26), religion-related (25), human services (23), community improvement and capacity building (17), philanthropy, voluntarism and grantmaking foundations (16). By type of exemption the largest groups are charitable, educational, religious and similar organization (289), social and recreational club (32), civic league and social welfare organization (22). The share of Grand Forks in the state's 6,852 organizations is 6%.

How large they report themselves to be

All 412 organizations in Grand Forks carry an income code from the latest return. Of those, 3% report income under $25,000, 25% report $25,000 to $499,999, 13% report $500,000 to $4,999,999 and 5% report $5 million or more; 55% have income code 0, which the IRS table lists as $0; that covers organizations that reported no income and probably also organizations that have no return data in the file, so the file alone cannot tell the two apart. The bands are the IRS's own income codes for the most recent return, and a return can be a few years old. The IRS information sheet says amounts are missing for organizations that file only the small e-Postcard or that are not required to file.

Gifts, legal form and group ties

The file marks contributions as deductible for 309 of the organizations (75%) and as not deductible for 97 (24%). By type of organization in the file: 287 corporations, 113 associations, 9 trusts, 2 co-operatives. 310 are independent organizations and 100 are subordinate units in a group ruling, such as a local branch of a larger body.

The foundation code, which the IRS uses for 501(c)(3) organizations, marks 206 organizations that get a substantial part of their support from the public or government, 36 churches, 22 private foundations, 16 supporting organizations, 2 hospitals or medical research organizations. These are the IRS's own classes and the file does not describe an organization's work.

When the exemption was recognised

The ruling date is the month and year of the IRS letter that recognised the exemption, which is not the year the organization was founded. 117 of the 412 organizations with a ruling date (28%) were recognised in 2016 or later. The decades with the most rulings are the 2020s (88), the 2010s (69), the 1970s (54).

By filing requirement code: 151 must file Form 990 or 990-EZ, 199 need only file the small Form 990-N (income under $50,000 a year, in the IRS's words), and 60 are marked as not required to file (churches, religious organizations, government bodies and others).
