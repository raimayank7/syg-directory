Tax-exempt organizations in Logan, Utah

248 organizations

The IRS file has 248 tax-exempt organizations with an address in Logan, Utah.

What kinds of organizations

By field of work, 47 have no code and the coded ones are mostly human services (32), arts, culture and humanities (30), education (27), philanthropy, voluntarism and grantmaking foundations (23), recreation and sports (17), environment (9), community improvement and capacity building (8). By type of exemption the largest groups are charitable, educational, religious and similar organization (211), business league, chamber of commerce and board of trade (8), social and recreational club (7). The share of Logan in the state's 13,228 organizations is 2%.

How large they report themselves to be

All 248 organizations in Logan carry an income code from the latest return. Of those, 7% report income under $25,000, 26% report $25,000 to $499,999, 11% report $500,000 to $4,999,999 and 4% report $5 million or more; 52% have income code 0, which the IRS table lists as $0; that covers organizations that reported no income and probably also organizations that have no return data in the file, so the file alone cannot tell the two apart. The bands are the IRS's own income codes for the most recent return, and a return can be a few years old. The IRS information sheet says amounts are missing for organizations that file only the small e-Postcard or that are not required to file.

Gifts, legal form and group ties

The file marks contributions as deductible for 210 of the organizations (85%) and as not deductible for 34 (14%). By type of organization in the file: 207 corporations, 32 associations, 7 trusts. 216 are independent organizations and 32 are subordinate units in a group ruling, such as a local branch of a larger body.

The foundation code, which the IRS uses for 501(c)(3) organizations, marks 146 organizations that get a substantial part of their support from the public or government, 28 supporting organizations, 19 private foundations, 12 churches, 3 schools. These are the IRS's own classes and the file does not describe an organization's work.

When the exemption was recognised

The ruling date is the month and year of the IRS letter that recognised the exemption, which is not the year the organization was founded. 111 of the 248 organizations with a ruling date (45%) were recognised in 2016 or later. The decades with the most rulings are the 2020s (80), the 2010s (67), the 1990s (29).

By filing requirement code: 89 must file Form 990 or 990-EZ, 123 need only file the small Form 990-N (income under $50,000 a year, in the IRS's words), and 36 are marked as not required to file (churches, religious organizations, government bodies and others).
