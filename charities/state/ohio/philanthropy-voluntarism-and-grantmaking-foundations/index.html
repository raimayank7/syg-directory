Philanthropy, voluntarism and grantmaking foundations organizations in Ohio

3,608 organizations

The IRS file has 3,608 philanthropy, voluntarism and grantmaking foundations organizations in Ohio (NTEE major group T): 4.7% of the state's 77,220 tax-exempt organizations. For 3,424 of them the IRS has amounts: together they report $13.84 billion of income.

How large they report themselves to be

All 3,608 philanthropy, voluntarism and grantmaking foundations organizations in Ohio carry an income code from the latest return. Of those, 11% report income under $25,000, 35% report $25,000 to $499,999, 20% report $500,000 to $4,999,999 and 6% report $5 million or more; 28% have income code 0, which the IRS table lists as $0; that covers organizations that reported no income and probably also organizations that have no return data in the file, so the file alone cannot tell the two apart. The bands are the IRS's own income codes for the most recent return, and a return can be a few years old. The IRS information sheet says amounts are missing for organizations that file only the small e-Postcard or that are not required to file.

Gifts, legal form and group ties

The file marks contributions as deductible for 3,577 of the organizations (99%) and as not deductible for 25 (1%). By type of organization in the file: 2,750 corporations, 723 trusts, 132 associations. 3,575 are independent organizations and 21 are subordinate units in a group ruling, such as a local branch of a larger body.

The foundation code, which the IRS uses for 501(c)(3) organizations, marks 1,890 private foundations, 1,490 organizations that get a substantial part of their support from the public or government, 190 supporting organizations, 5 churches. These are the IRS's own classes and the file does not describe an organization's work.

When the exemption was recognised

The ruling date is the month and year of the IRS letter that recognised the exemption, which is not the year the organization was founded. 1,464 of the 3,608 organizations with a ruling date (41%) were recognised in 2016 or later. The decades with the most rulings are the 2010s (1,023), the 2020s (956), the 2000s (939).

By filing requirement code: 669 must file Form 990 or 990-EZ, 1,036 need only file the small Form 990-N (income under $50,000 a year, in the IRS's words), and 1,903 are marked as not required to file (churches, religious organizations, government bodies and others).

Types of exemption and towns

By IRS subsection, these organizations are 3,581 charitable, educational, religious and similar organization, 18 civic league and social welfare organization, 3 domestic fraternal society. The towns with the most of them are Cincinnati (553), Columbus (331), Cleveland (203), Brooklyn (144), Akron (81). Counts are of organizations at the address on file with the IRS.
