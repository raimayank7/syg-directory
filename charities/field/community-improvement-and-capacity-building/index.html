Community improvement and capacity building organizations in the United States

73,578 organizations

The IRS file has 73,578 community improvement and capacity building organizations across the United States (NTEE major group S), 3.7% of the 1,975,209 counted here. For 65,577 of them the IRS has amounts: together they report $119.03 billion of income.

Types of exemption in this field

By IRS subsection, the community improvement and capacity building organizations in this field are 46,555 charitable, educational, religious and similar organization, 19,322 business league, chamber of commerce and board of trade, 5,332 civic league and social welfare organization, 1,143 title-holding corporation. A field of work says what an organization does, while the subsection says under which part of the tax code it is exempt, so the same field can appear under several types.

How large they report themselves to be

All 73,578 community improvement and capacity building organizations carry an income code from the latest return. Of those, 4% report income under $25,000, 23% report $25,000 to $499,999, 9% report $500,000 to $4,999,999 and 2% report $5 million or more; 61% have income code 0, which the IRS table lists as $0; that covers organizations that reported no income and probably also organizations that have no return data in the file, so the file alone cannot tell the two apart. The bands are the IRS's own income codes for the most recent return, and a return can be a few years old. The IRS information sheet says amounts are missing for organizations that file only the small e-Postcard or that are not required to file.

Gifts, legal form and group ties

The file marks contributions as deductible for 46,777 of the organizations (64%) and as not deductible for 26,473 (36%). By type of organization in the file: 66,526 corporations, 6,588 associations, 367 trusts, 6 co-operatives. 71,057 are independent organizations and 2,217 are subordinate units in a group ruling, such as a local branch of a larger body.

The foundation code, which the IRS uses for 501(c)(3) organizations, marks 44,029 organizations that get a substantial part of their support from the public or government, 1,444 private foundations, 953 supporting organizations, 60 churches, 15 schools. These are the IRS's own classes and the file does not describe an organization's work.

When the exemption was recognised

The ruling date is the month and year of the IRS letter that recognised the exemption, which is not the year the organization was founded. 41,464 of the 73,578 organizations with a ruling date (56%) were recognised in 2016 or later. The decades with the most rulings are the 2020s (30,540), the 2010s (23,423), the 2000s (11,496).

By filing requirement code: 24,396 must file Form 990 or 990-EZ, 47,143 need only file the small Form 990-N (income under $50,000 a year, in the IRS's words), and 2,023 are marked as not required to file (churches, religious organizations, government bodies and others).
