Public schools in Marietta, Georgia

60 schools

The Common Core of Data 2023-24 lists 60 public schools in Marietta, Georgia: 37 elementary, 12 middle, 10 high, 1 prekindergarten. 1 of them (1.7%) are charter schools. Besides regular schools there are 1 alternative schools. The file reports 53,072 students in 60 of them; the median school has 706 students and the largest has 2,773. Grades offered run from prekindergarten to grade 12.

They belong to 3 school districts: Cobb County (46), Marietta City (13), Department of Juvenile Justice (1).

Marietta is the 2nd largest town in Georgia by number of public schools in this file, out of 371 towns, and holds 2.58% of the state's 2,327 schools.

Which grades the schools offer

The directory marks, for each school, every grade it offers. Counting those marks for Marietta: 37 of the 60 schools offer prekindergarten and 36 offer kindergarten. For grades 1 to 5 the counts run from 36 to 38 schools per grade, for grades 6 to 8 from 13 to 13, and for grades 9 to 12 from 10 to 10. Every grade from 1 to 12 is offered by at least one school in the list. A school that offers a wide span, such as kindergarten to grade 12, is counted in every grade it covers, so the counts add up to more than the number of schools. The counts come from the directory's yes and no marks and describe what schools are listed as offering, not how many seats are open.

How large the schools are

The membership file gives a student count for 60 of the 60 schools of Marietta. The middle school has 706 students; a quarter of them have 546 or fewer and a quarter have 1,026 or more. The smallest count is 4 (George W. Hartmann Center) and the largest is 2,773 (Osborne High School). 2 schools have fewer than 100 students. Counts are the total membership of each school in the 2023-24 school year as the file reports it, with adult education left out. They describe size, and nothing about the quality of teaching or results.

Who authorises the charter schools

For the charter schools in this list the directory names the body that granted the charter: Cobb County (1). A charter school is publicly funded and free to attend, but it is run under an agreement with its authoriser instead of directly by a district board.
