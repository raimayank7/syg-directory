Public schools in Asheboro, North Carolina

16 schools

The Common Core of Data 2023-24 lists 16 public schools in Asheboro, North Carolina: 9 elementary, 3 high, 3 middle, 1 other. 1 of them (6.2%) are charter schools. The file reports 9,379 students in 16 of them; the median school has 454 students and the largest has 2,152. Grades offered run from prekindergarten to grade 13.

They belong to 3 school districts: Asheboro City Schools (8), Randolph County School System (7), Uwharrie Charter Academy (1).

Asheboro is the 32nd largest town in North Carolina by number of public schools in this file, out of 498 towns, and holds 0.59% of the state's 2,716 schools.

Which grades the schools offer

The directory marks, for each school, every grade it offers. Counting those marks for Asheboro: 3 of the 16 schools offer prekindergarten and 10 offer kindergarten. For grades 1 to 5 the counts run from 10 to 10 schools per grade, for grades 6 to 8 from 5 to 5, and for grades 9 to 12 from 4 to 4. Every grade from 1 to 12 is offered by at least one school in the list. A school that offers a wide span, such as kindergarten to grade 12, is counted in every grade it covers, so the counts add up to more than the number of schools. The counts come from the directory's yes and no marks and describe what schools are listed as offering, not how many seats are open.

How large the schools are

The membership file gives a student count for 16 of the 16 schools of Asheboro. The middle school has 454 students; a quarter of them have 345 or fewer and a quarter have 573 or more. The smallest count is 102 (The Virtual Academy at Randolph) and the largest is 2,152 (Uwharrie Charter Academy). Counts are the total membership of each school in the 2023-24 school year as the file reports it, with adult education left out. They describe size, and nothing about the quality of teaching or results.

Who authorises the charter schools

For the charter schools in this list the directory names the body that granted the charter: Nc State Board Of Education (1). A charter school is publicly funded and free to attend, but it is run under an agreement with its authoriser instead of directly by a district board.
