Public schools in Key West, Florida

15 schools

The Common Core of Data 2023-24 lists 15 public schools in Key West, Florida: 8 high, 6 elementary, 1 other. 4 of them (26.7%) are charter schools. Besides regular schools there are 4 alternative schools. The file reports 4,321 students in 15 of them; the median school has 49 students and the largest has 1,302. Grades offered run from prekindergarten to grade 12.

They belong to 1 school district: Monroe (15).

Key West is the 63rd largest town in Florida by number of public schools in this file, out of 439 towns, and holds 0.35% of the state's 4,229 schools.

Which grades the schools offer

The directory marks, for each school, every grade it offers. Counting those marks for Key West: 4 of the 15 schools offer prekindergarten and 7 offer kindergarten. For grades 1 to 5 the counts run from 7 to 7 schools per grade, for grades 6 to 8 from 7 to 9, and for grades 9 to 12 from 9 to 9. Every grade from 1 to 12 is offered by at least one school in the list. A school that offers a wide span, such as kindergarten to grade 12, is counted in every grade it covers, so the counts add up to more than the number of schools. The counts come from the directory's yes and no marks and describe what schools are listed as offering, not how many seats are open.

How large the schools are

The membership file gives a student count for 15 of the 15 schools of Key West. The middle school has 49 students; a quarter of them have 2 or fewer and a quarter have 552 or more. The smallest count is 0 (Monroe Juvenile Detention Center) and the largest is 1,302 (Key West High School). 10 schools have fewer than 100 students. Counts are the total membership of each school in the 2023-24 school year as the file reports it, with adult education left out. They describe size, and nothing about the quality of teaching or results.

Who authorises the charter schools

For the charter schools in this list the directory names the body that granted the charter: Monroe District Office (4). A charter school is publicly funded and free to attend, but it is run under an agreement with its authoriser instead of directly by a district board.
