Public schools in Coral Springs, Florida

31 schools

The Common Core of Data 2023-24 lists 31 public schools in Coral Springs, Florida: 17 elementary, 7 high, 7 middle. 11 of them (35.5%) are charter schools. Besides regular schools there are 2 alternative schools. The file reports 27,719 students in 31 of them; the median school has 729 students and the largest has 2,744. Grades offered run from prekindergarten to grade 12.

They belong to 2 school districts: Broward (30), Hillsborough (1).

Coral Springs is the 23rd largest town in Florida by number of public schools in this file, out of 439 towns, and holds 0.73% of the state's 4,229 schools.

Which grades the schools offer

The directory marks, for each school, every grade it offers. Counting those marks for Coral Springs: 12 of the 31 schools offer prekindergarten and 17 offer kindergarten. For grades 1 to 5 the counts run from 17 to 17 schools per grade, for grades 6 to 8 from 11 to 11, and for grades 9 to 12 from 7 to 7. Every grade from 1 to 12 is offered by at least one school in the list. A school that offers a wide span, such as kindergarten to grade 12, is counted in every grade it covers, so the counts add up to more than the number of schools. The counts come from the directory's yes and no marks and describe what schools are listed as offering, not how many seats are open.

How large the schools are

The membership file gives a student count for 31 of the 31 schools of Coral Springs. The middle school has 729 students; a quarter of them have 443 or fewer and a quarter have 1,057 or more. The smallest count is 57 (Eagles Nest Middle Charter School) and the largest is 2,744 (Coral Glades High School). 1 school has fewer than 100 students. Counts are the total membership of each school in the 2023-24 school year as the file reports it, with adult education left out. They describe size, and nothing about the quality of teaching or results.

Who authorises the charter schools

For the charter schools in this list the directory names the body that granted the charter: Broward District Office (10), Hillsborough District Office (1). A charter school is publicly funded and free to attend, but it is run under an agreement with its authoriser instead of directly by a district board.
