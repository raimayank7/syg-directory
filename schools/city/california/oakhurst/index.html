Public schools in Oakhurst, California

9 schools

The Common Core of Data 2023-24 lists 9 public schools in Oakhurst, California: 5 high, 2 elementary, 1 other, 1 middle. 2 of them (22.2%) are charter schools. Besides regular schools there are 4 alternative schools. The file reports 1,490 students in 9 of them; the median school has 82 students and the largest has 557. Grades offered run from kindergarten to grade 12.

They belong to 4 school districts: Yosemite Unified (5), Bass Lake Joint Union Elementary (2), Glacier High School Charter District (1), Mountain Home Charter (Alternative) District (1).

Oakhurst is the 302nd largest town in California by number of public schools in this file, out of 1,010 towns, and holds 0.09% of the state's 10,326 schools.

Which grades the schools offer

The directory marks, for each school, every grade it offers. Counting those marks for Oakhurst: 0 of the 9 schools offer prekindergarten and 3 offer kindergarten. For grades 1 to 5 the counts run from 3 to 3 schools per grade, for grades 6 to 8 from 3 to 3, and for grades 9 to 12 from 6 to 6. Every grade from 1 to 12 is offered by at least one school in the list. A school that offers a wide span, such as kindergarten to grade 12, is counted in every grade it covers, so the counts add up to more than the number of schools. The counts come from the directory's yes and no marks and describe what schools are listed as offering, not how many seats are open.

How large the schools are

The membership file gives a student count for 9 of the 9 schools of Oakhurst. The middle school has 82 students; a quarter of them have 33 or fewer and a quarter have 214 or more. The smallest count is 0 (Campbell High Community Day) and the largest is 557 (Yosemite High). 5 schools have fewer than 100 students. Counts are the total membership of each school in the 2023-24 school year as the file reports it, with adult education left out. They describe size, and nothing about the quality of teaching or results.

Who authorises the charter schools

For the charter schools in this list the directory names the body that granted the charter: Glacier High School Charter District (1), Mountain Home Charter (Alternative) District (1). A charter school is publicly funded and free to attend, but it is run under an agreement with its authoriser instead of directly by a district board.
