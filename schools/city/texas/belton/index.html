Public schools in Belton, Texas

14 schools

The Common Core of Data 2023-24 lists 14 public schools in Belton, Texas: 7 elementary, 3 high, 2 middle, 1 prekindergarten, 1 secondary. 2 of them (14.3%) are charter schools. Besides regular schools there are 1 alternative schools. The file reports 7,244 students in 14 of them; the median school has 458 students and the largest has 1,836. Grades offered run from prekindergarten to grade 12.

They belong to 3 school districts: Belton Isd (12), University Of Texas University Charter School (1), Orenda Charter School (1).

Belton is the 106th largest town in Texas by number of public schools in this file, out of 1,088 towns, and holds 0.15% of the state's 9,217 schools.

Which grades the schools offer

The directory marks, for each school, every grade it offers. Counting those marks for Belton: 1 of the 14 schools offers prekindergarten and 7 offer kindergarten. For grades 1 to 5 the counts run from 7 to 7 schools per grade, for grades 6 to 8 from 3 to 4, and for grades 9 to 12 from 3 to 4. Every grade from 1 to 12 is offered by at least one school in the list. A school that offers a wide span, such as kindergarten to grade 12, is counted in every grade it covers, so the counts add up to more than the number of schools. The counts come from the directory's yes and no marks and describe what schools are listed as offering, not how many seats are open.

How large the schools are

The membership file gives a student count for 14 of the 14 schools of Belton. The middle school has 458 students; a quarter of them have 219 or fewer and a quarter have 676 or more. The smallest count is 0 (Belton New Tech H S At Waskow) and the largest is 1,836 (Belton H S). 2 schools have fewer than 100 students. Counts are the total membership of each school in the 2023-24 school year as the file reports it, with adult education left out. They describe size, and nothing about the quality of teaching or results.

Who authorises the charter schools

For the charter schools in this list the directory names the body that granted the charter: State Board Of Education (2). A charter school is publicly funded and free to attend, but it is run under an agreement with its authoriser instead of directly by a district board.
