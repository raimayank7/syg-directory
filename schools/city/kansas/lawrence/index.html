Public schools in Lawrence, Kansas

18 schools

The Common Core of Data 2023-24 lists 18 public schools in Lawrence, Kansas: 11 elementary, 4 middle, 2 high, 1 other. 1 of them (5.6%) are charter schools. The file reports 10,592 students in 18 of them; the median school has 438 students and the largest has 1,768. Grades offered run from prekindergarten to grade 12.

They belong to 1 school district: Lawrence (18).

Lawrence is the 7th largest town in Kansas by number of public schools in this file, out of 370 towns, and holds 1.34% of the state's 1,348 schools.

Which grades the schools offer

The directory marks, for each school, every grade it offers. Counting those marks for Lawrence: 1 of the 18 schools offers prekindergarten and 12 offer kindergarten. For grades 1 to 5 the counts run from 12 to 12 schools per grade, for grades 6 to 8 from 5 to 5, and for grades 9 to 12 from 3 to 3. Every grade from 1 to 12 is offered by at least one school in the list. A school that offers a wide span, such as kindergarten to grade 12, is counted in every grade it covers, so the counts add up to more than the number of schools. The counts come from the directory's yes and no marks and describe what schools are listed as offering, not how many seats are open.

How large the schools are

The membership file gives a student count for 18 of the 18 schools of Lawrence. The middle school has 438 students; a quarter of them have 398 or fewer and a quarter have 626 or more. The smallest count is 238 (Woodlawn Elem) and the largest is 1,768 (Lawrence Free State High). Counts are the total membership of each school in the 2023-24 school year as the file reports it, with adult education left out. They describe size, and nothing about the quality of teaching or results.

Who authorises the charter schools

For the charter schools in this list the directory names the body that granted the charter: Kansas State Board Of Education (1). A charter school is publicly funded and free to attend, but it is run under an agreement with its authoriser instead of directly by a district board.
