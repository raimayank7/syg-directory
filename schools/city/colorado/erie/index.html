Public schools in Erie, Colorado

9 schools

The Common Core of Data 2023-24 lists 9 public schools in Erie, Colorado: 7 elementary, 1 middle, 1 high. 1 of them (11.1%) are charter schools. The file reports 7,200 students in 9 of them; the median school has 614 students and the largest has 1,759. Grades offered run from prekindergarten to grade 12.

They belong to 2 school districts: St. Vrain Valley School District No. Re1J (8), Boulder Valley School District No. Re2 (1).

Erie is the 33rd largest town in Colorado by number of public schools in this file, out of 270 towns, and holds 0.47% of the state's 1,908 schools.

Which grades the schools offer

The directory marks, for each school, every grade it offers. Counting those marks for Erie: 6 of the 9 schools offer prekindergarten and 7 offer kindergarten. For grades 1 to 5 the counts run from 7 to 7 schools per grade, for grades 6 to 8 from 4 to 4, and for grades 9 to 12 from 1 to 1. Every grade from 1 to 12 is offered by at least one school in the list. A school that offers a wide span, such as kindergarten to grade 12, is counted in every grade it covers, so the counts add up to more than the number of schools. The counts come from the directory's yes and no marks and describe what schools are listed as offering, not how many seats are open.

How large the schools are

The membership file gives a student count for 9 of the 9 schools of Erie. The middle school has 614 students; a quarter of them have 548 or fewer and a quarter have 754 or more. The smallest count is 450 (Erie Elementary School) and the largest is 1,759 (Erie High School). Counts are the total membership of each school in the 2023-24 school year as the file reports it, with adult education left out. They describe size, and nothing about the quality of teaching or results.

Who authorises the charter schools

For the charter schools in this list the directory names the body that granted the charter: St. Vrain Valley School District No. Re1J (1). A charter school is publicly funded and free to attend, but it is run under an agreement with its authoriser instead of directly by a district board.
