Antelope Valley Union High: public schools

13 schools

California, school district

The Common Core of Data 2023-24 lists 13 public schools in the Antelope Valley Union High district in California: 13 high. 1 of them (7.7%) are charter schools. Besides regular schools there are 4 alternative schools. The file reports 21,716 students in 13 of them; the median school has 1,526 students and the largest has 3,170. Grades offered run from grade 6 to grade 12.

This is the 189th largest of the 2,048 districts in California by number of public schools, and holds 0.13% of the state's 10,326 schools in this file.

Its schools are in 4 towns, most in Lancaster (6), Palmdale (4), Quartz Hill (2), Littlerock (1).

Which grades the schools offer

The directory marks, for each school, every grade it offers. Counting those marks for this district: 0 of the 13 schools offer prekindergarten and 0 offer kindergarten. For grades 1 to 5 the counts run from 0 to 0 schools per grade, for grades 6 to 8 from 1 to 1, and for grades 9 to 12 from 13 to 13. No school in this list offers grade 1, grade 2, grade 3, grade 4 or grade 5. That can happen when a district sends those students to schools run by another district, to a state or county school, or to a charter school; the directory does not say which, so ask the district. A school that offers a wide span, such as kindergarten to grade 12, is counted in every grade it covers, so the counts add up to more than the number of schools. The counts come from the directory's yes and no marks and describe what schools are listed as offering, not how many seats are open.

How large the schools are

The membership file gives a student count for 13 of the 13 schools of this district. The middle school has 1,526 students; a quarter of them have 646 or fewer and a quarter have 2,592 or more. The smallest count is 43 (Phoenix High Community Day) and the largest is 3,170 (Quartz Hill High). 1 school has fewer than 100 students. Counts are the total membership of each school in the 2023-24 school year as the file reports it, with adult education left out. They describe size, and nothing about the quality of teaching or results.

Who authorises the charter schools

For the charter schools in this list the directory names the body that granted the charter: Antelope Valley Union High (1). A charter school is publicly funded and free to attend, but it is run under an agreement with its authoriser instead of directly by a district board.

Where the schools are
