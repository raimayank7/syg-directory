Cusd 308: public schools

22 schools

Illinois, school district

The Common Core of Data 2023-24 lists 22 public schools in the Cusd 308 district in Illinois: 13 elementary, 5 middle, 2 high, 1 prekindergarten, 1 other. Besides regular schools there are 1 alternative schools. The file reports 16,851 students in 22 of them; the median school has 594 students and the largest has 2,866. Grades offered run from prekindergarten to grade 12.

This is the 12th largest of the 960 districts in Illinois by number of public schools, and holds 0.50% of the state's 4,400 schools in this file.

Its schools are in 4 towns, most in Oswego (13), Aurora (4), Montgomery (3), Plainfield (2).

Which grades the schools offer

The directory marks, for each school, every grade it offers. Counting those marks for this district: 4 of the 22 schools offer prekindergarten and 14 offer kindergarten. For grades 1 to 5 the counts run from 14 to 14 schools per grade, for grades 6 to 8 from 6 to 6, and for grades 9 to 12 from 3 to 3. Every grade from 1 to 12 is offered by at least one school in the list. A school that offers a wide span, such as kindergarten to grade 12, is counted in every grade it covers, so the counts add up to more than the number of schools. The counts come from the directory's yes and no marks and describe what schools are listed as offering, not how many seats are open.

How large the schools are

The membership file gives a student count for 22 of the 22 schools of this district. The middle school has 594 students; a quarter of them have 447 or fewer and a quarter have 688 or more. The smallest count is 0 (East View Academy School) and the largest is 2,866 (Oswego High School). 1 school has fewer than 100 students. Counts are the total membership of each school in the 2023-24 school year as the file reports it, with adult education left out. They describe size, and nothing about the quality of teaching or results.

Where the schools are

The schools' street addresses fall in 4 ZIP codes, the most schools in 60543 (13). This is where the buildings stand, which is not always the same as the area the district serves.
